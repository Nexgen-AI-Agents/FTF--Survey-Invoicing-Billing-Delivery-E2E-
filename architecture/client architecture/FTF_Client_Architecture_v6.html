FTF Invoice Pipeline

How NexGen turns a finished survey into an emailed invoice — automatically, with one human OK
⏱  Runs by itself every 30 minutes  |  on the NexGen server
In one breath

A helper robot wakes up every 30 minutes, finds survey orders that are ready to bill, works out a fair price, and writes each one as a row in a shared Excel sheet. A person at NexGen looks at the row, fixes anything in the blue boxes if needed, and picks Approve, Reject, or On‑hold. Only when a human clicks Approve does the robot create the real invoice and email it to the client — and it can never send the same invoice twice.

Step 1 — Find work
Look for orders that need an invoice
Every 30 minutes the robot checks FTF (the survey system) for orders marked with the $ “needs invoice” flag. It only looks at new ones it hasn’t seen before.
Like checking your mailbox for new letters — only opening the ones you haven’t read yet.

For each order it gathers who the client is, the property address, and which survey services were done — everything an invoice needs.

Price the invoice fairly
The robot suggests a price using the last 2 years of real invoices for similar jobs in the same area, plus everything it has learned from past human corrections and any fixed Pricing Rules the team set.
Like pricing a lemonade stand by remembering what people happily paid before.

Step 6 — Send (Approve only)
Create the invoice & email the client
The robot makes the invoice in FTF and FTF emails it to the client with the PDF attached. It uses your blue breakdown, not its own guess.
Sends exactly once. If the website is down mid‑way, it safely tries again later — it will never email the same invoice twice.

Step 7 — Learn
Get smarter for next time
The robot remembers how you changed the breakdown, what you wrote in Notes / Learning, and whether you approved or rejected — so its next prices are closer to what you want.
Like a new teammate who remembers your feedback and needs less correcting each week.
↻  Then it loops — back to Step 1 on the next 30‑minute run

The two big safety promises
1. A human is always the gate. No invoice is created and no email is sent until a person clicks Approve on the sheet.
2. Never twice. If FTF or the internet hiccups while making or sending an invoice, the robot picks up safely on the next run — an invoice is created at most once and a client is emailed at most once. If it’s ever unsure whether an email went out, it pauses that one for a person to confirm instead of risking a duplicate.
